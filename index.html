About Me

I am an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at the Stevens Institute of Technology in Hoboken, NJ. My research centers around the semantic foundations of program logics and applications of formal methods to improve real world software quality.

I received my PhD from Cornell University in 2026 where I was advised by Alexandra Silva. Before Cornell, I was a staff software engineer in the Facebook Programming Languages and Runtimes team, where I applied formal methods by using dependently typed Haskell in production and formally verifying concurrent algorithms for an OS microkernel. My research has been recognized with the 2024 ACM SIGPLAN John Vlissides Award for applied software research and a distinguished paper award at POPL 2026.

I am recruiting PhD students for the 2027-28 academic year. If you are interested in working with me, please reach out and apply to Stevens!

Research

The overarching goal of my research is to develop mathematical foundations for trustworthy software systems. I combine formal reasoning, automated analysis, and AI to make complex programs more reliable. My work spans semantic foundations, scalable program analysis techniques, and infrastructure for AI-assisted verification. I am currently working on the following research directions:

Mathematical Foundations of Program Logics Foundations for reasoning about complex programs, including challenging combinations of effects such as concurrency, probability, and nondeterminism.
Next-Generation Static Analysis Scalable analysis techniques enhanced by generative AI to solve targeted reasoning problems while preserving the efficiency of traditional methods.
Infrastructure for AI-Based Program Verification Trusted formal libraries and verification frameworks that enable AI systems to reason about programs with mathematical rigor.
